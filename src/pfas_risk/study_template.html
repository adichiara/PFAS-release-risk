PFAS in Massachusetts private wells

About __PRIVATE_POP__ residents live outside community water service and drink from private wells that no public program tests. This study estimates, for every census block on private wells, the probability that groundwater there has PFAS6 at or above the 20 ng/L state standard: a model trained on public-well results, checked against and calibrated to MassDEP's private-well testing. Public data only.

Raw-water samples are tied to a source by their sampling-point code (RW-03G becomes <system>-03G), or, for systems with a single groundwater source, to that source. The drinking-water API returns at most 100 results per system and water type, so heavily sampled systems are represented by a sample of their results; the label is a well's maximum, which is robust to that.

2. Groundwater model

A logistic regression (L2, C = 0.1, standardized log1p features) predicts whether a public well's highest raw PFAS6 reached 20 ng/L, and separately whether PFAS6 was detected. Features, all measured at the well:

  • Nearby measured PFAS: the inverse-distance-squared weighted mean of log(1 + PFAS6) at public wells within 5 km, excluding the well's own water system (distance floored at 200 m), and the number of such wells.
  • Development: population within 1 km (census blocks), whether the point is inside a sewer service area (outside it, homes use septic systems), and whether it is over a high- or medium-yield aquifer.

Validation holds out whole towns (5 folds, 5 repeats), so each well is scored by a model fit without its town. The nearby-PFAS feature never uses the well's own system, which keeps a system's other wells (often a few hundred meters away, on the same aquifer) from standing in for the answer. Applied statewide, the same features are computed at each private-well census block's representative point.

What did not help. Counts of and distances to mapped hazard sources (EPA facilities in PFAS-related industries, MassDEP major facilities, hazardous-waste generators, underground storage tanks, fire stations, airports, military installations, landfills, TRI reporters), measured within 1 and 3 km of a well or inside its Zone II recharge area, left ROC AUC within 0.03 of development alone; gradient boosting was no better than the logistic model. Groundwater PFAS at public wells is spatially clustered and tracks development, but mapped source locations do not explain which wells exceed the standard.

3. Check against private-well testing

MassDEP's program tested private wells in towns where most residents use them and published each town's count of wells sampled and wells at 20 ng/L or more. The model never sees these results, so they test whether a model learned on public wells transfers to private ones.

4. Calibration and blending

Calibration. One shift in the model's log-odds, fit by maximum likelihood to the towns' counts, where a town's rate is the population-weighted mean over its private-well blocks:

p_cal = σ( logit(p_model) + β ), β = __OFFSET__ k_t ~ Binomial( n_t, mean over town t of p_cal )

Adding a slope (σ(a·logit p + β)) did not improve leave-one-town-out log loss, so the offset alone is used.

Blending. In tested towns the calibrated estimate is combined with the town's own result, with the model acting as a prior worth m wells; m is fit by beta-binomial maximum likelihood. Each block's log-odds in the town are then shifted so the town's mean equals the blend:

r_t = ( k_t + m · p̄_t ) / ( n_t + m ), m = __PRIOR__ wells

A town with 40 tested wells therefore leans about __BLEND_WEIGHT__ on its own results. Untested towns keep the calibrated model estimate.

6. Limitations

  • Estimates are area-level probabilities from a model with modest discrimination; they cannot identify individual contaminated wells.
  • Calibration rests on __PW_TOWNS__ towns chosen because most residents use private wells, and on invitations mostly targeted near suspected sources; rates in other settings may differ.
  • The outcome is PFAS6 against the 20 ng/L state standard. EPA's 2024 limits of 4 ng/L each for PFOA and PFOS are stricter and would classify more wells.
  • Private-well status is inferred from community water service areas; homes inside a service area that still use wells, and service-area boundary errors, are not captured.
  • Public-well results run through __DATA_DATE__ and the private-well program sampled in 2020 to 2022; groundwater PFAS can change over time.
