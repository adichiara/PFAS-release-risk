PFAS release risk in Massachusetts

Relative risk of a reported PFAS release across the state, on a grid of 21,774 1 km² hexagons, built only from public data.

Preliminary. Trained on 194 located PFAS releases from MassDEP's release database plus the 2021 project list. Scores rank areas relative to each other. They are not probabilities of contamination.

Who lives in higher-risk areas

Each 2020 census block takes the score of the 1 km² hexagon it sits in (area-weighted when it straddles cells), so the question becomes how many people live in the areas the model ranks highest. Groups come from the state's 2020 Environmental Justice block groups (minority, income and English-isolation criteria) and from community water service areas: residents outside them are presumed to be on private wells. "vs density" divides a group's share by the share expected from where it lives on the density scale alone (1.00 means no difference beyond density; population density is a model input, and many demographics follow it).

About 127,000 people on private wells live in the top-risk 10% of land; a release nearby matters most for them, since their water is not tested or treated by a public system. Scores describe the area, not any one home, and reflect where releases have been reported, which also depends on where testing and investigations happen.

What drives the scores

How much the model's held-out ROC AUC drops when one group of features is shuffled (count inside, count within 2 km and distance to the nearest site together). Larger drops mean the model leans on that group more. Correlated groups share credit: industrial sites cluster together, so shuffling one sector leaves others carrying similar information. This shows association with reported releases, not cause.

Drinking-water PFAS uses only samples collected before 2023. Some releases were found through drinking-water testing and filed at the supply well, so part of this group's weight is recognizing those. The forward test, which scores only releases reported after the cutoff, is the check that it still helps.

Compared with other units

The same features, validation and permutation test on other spatial units. Census block groups range from under 0.1 km² to over 200 km², so land area alone explains much of which ones contain a reported release; equal-area hexagons remove that effect, which is why they lead this page. Smaller cells rank better: with the same 10% of land, 1 km² cells capture more releases than 2 km² or 4 km² cells. Average precision depends on how many cells contain a release, so compare it within a row, not between rows.

Reported PFAS releases by year

All 214 release sites on the list, by the year MassDEP was notified. PFAS reporting grew after Massachusetts set its PFAS6 drinking water standard and cleanup rules in 2019-2020, so the record is short and still filling in.

Method

  • Unit: equal-area 1 km² hexagons clipped to the state (Massachusetts State Plane meters). Population, housing and land area come from 2020 census blocks, weighted by the share of each block in a cell; each cell belongs to the town holding most of its land.
  • Response: MassDEP reportable releases (RTNs) with PFAS as a chemical, located by matching addresses to MassGIS address points.
  • Features: counts inside, counts within 2 km and distance to the nearest fire station, MassDEP major facility, hazardous-waste generator, air-permitted facility and underground storage tank, and to EPA-registered facilities in eight PFAS-related industries (textiles, paper, chemicals and plastics, metal finishing, electronics, petroleum, aviation and military, waste and wastewater); public water supply wells and intakes tested for PFAS6, with PFAS6 detected, and at or above the 20 ng/L standard, using only samples collected before 2023 so the forward test stays honest; landfills; aquifers; major-road density; population and housing density. Airports, military installations, EPA Toxics Release Inventory reporters and sewer service areas were tested and left out because they did not improve the model (see Data sources).
  • Validation: five-fold cross-validation holding out whole towns, a size- and density-matched permutation test, and a forward-in-time test on releases reported after a cutoff.
  • Map scores are out-of-fold: each cell is colored by a model trained without its town, so known release sites are not simply echoed back.
