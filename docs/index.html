Public water: measured

Every community water system had to test its treated water for PFAS6 (six PFAS compounds) under the 2020 state standard of 20 ng/L, so these are measurements, not estimates. Towns that buy their water, such as the MWRA communities around Boston, take their supplier's results. "Now" is each system's average over its last 12 months of results (for small systems that tested less often after low early results, that can be 2021 or 2022); "highest year" is its worst calendar-year average, often before treatment was installed.

Private wells: estimated

About 1.0 million people live outside every community water service area and are presumed to drink from private wells, which no public program tests. Their risk is estimated from public wells, which were tested systematically: a model trained on the highest raw (untreated) PFAS6 result at 1,030 public groundwater wells, using measured PFAS at other public wells within 5 km and how developed the area is (population, sewer service, aquifer).

ROC AUC is 0.5 for a coin flip and 1.0 for perfect ranking. The model beats development alone, but it cannot say which well is contaminated: groundwater PFAS here is widespread and depends on things public data does not capture (past spills, well depth, local geology). Distance to mapped industries, airports, landfills and other likely sources added nothing once nearby well results and development were included. Private wells also tend to be shallower than public wells, so the public-well rates may not carry over. Read these as area-level odds; only testing a well answers the question for a home.

Method

  • Public water: finished-water PFAS6 results per system (MassDEP drinking-water data on the EEA Data Portal; non-detects count as zero). Purchased water follows EPA SDWIS buyer-seller links. Census blocks take the community system whose service area holds them.
  • Private wells: census blocks outside every community service area. Estimates come from a logistic model trained on public groundwater wells and applied at each block.
  • Checks: state PFAS6 levels against EPA's UCMR 5 results; the groundwater model with town-grouped cross-validation.
  • Limits: the drinking-water API returns at most 100 results per system and water type, so about 40 large systems are represented by a sample of their results; small non-community systems (schools, workplaces) and bottled water are not modeled.

Reported releases

The companion analysis ranks where MassDEP is most likely to receive a PFAS release report. It reflects where testing and investigations happen as much as where PFAS is (metro Boston, on reservoir water with few wells to test, has almost no reported PFAS releases), so it is kept as supporting context: reported-release risk.
