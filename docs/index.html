PFAS release risk in Massachusetts

Relative risk of a reported PFAS release across the state, on a grid of 5,587 4 km² hexagons, built only from public data.

Preliminary. Trained on 194 located PFAS releases from MassDEP's release database plus the 2021 project list. Scores rank areas relative to each other. They are not probabilities of contamination.

What drives the scores

How much the model's held-out ROC AUC drops when one group of features is shuffled (count inside, count within 2 km and distance to the nearest site together). Larger drops mean the model leans on that group more. Correlated groups share credit: industrial sites cluster together, so shuffling one sector leaves others carrying similar information. This shows association with reported releases, not cause.

How well it validates

Five-fold cross-validation that holds out whole towns, repeated 10 times. "Smoothed" models blend each cell's score with its neighbors' within 3 km, computed inside each fold so no score depends on a model that saw that cell. The highlighted row is the model shown on the map.

Reported PFAS releases by year

All 214 release sites on the list, by the year MassDEP was notified. PFAS reporting grew after Massachusetts set its PFAS6 drinking water standard and cleanup rules in 2019-2020, so the record is short and still filling in.

Method

  • Unit: equal-area 4 km² hexagons clipped to the state (Massachusetts State Plane meters). Population, housing and land area come from 2020 census blocks, weighted by the share of each block in a cell; each cell belongs to the town holding most of its land.
  • Response: MassDEP reportable releases (RTNs) with PFAS as a chemical, located by matching addresses to MassGIS address points.
  • Features: counts inside, counts within 2 km and distance to the nearest fire station, MassDEP major facility, hazardous-waste generator, air-permitted facility and underground storage tank, and to EPA-registered facilities in eight PFAS-related industries (textiles, paper, chemicals and plastics, metal finishing, electronics, petroleum, aviation and military, waste and wastewater); landfills; aquifers; major-road density; population and housing density.
  • Validation: five-fold cross-validation holding out whole towns, a size- and density-matched permutation test, and a forward-in-time test on releases reported after a cutoff.
  • Map scores are out-of-fold: each cell is colored by a model trained without its town, so known release sites are not simply echoed back.
